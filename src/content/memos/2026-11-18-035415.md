    content: "An important lesson I have learned in my interactions with others is not to over-express feelings of apologetic guilt; doing so turns an otherwise natural relationship heavy. Phrases like \"I'm sorry,\" \"Sorry to bother you,\" \"You shouldn't have,\" or \"There was no need for this\" are polite when used sparingly. But repeating them constantly emphasizes indebtedness and sacrifice, placing a burden on the other person. Beyond their actual effort, they must then expend emotional energy to reassure the recipient and repeatedly prove that their gesture was truly voluntary—turning the relationship into a ledger of mutual debt. There are other ways to acknowledge someone's devotion. Put yourself in their shoes: when I do something for someone close to me, their joy is my reward. In this sense, rather than saying, \"I'm so deeply sorry for troubling you,\" a simple \"Thank you, you made me so happy\" is far lighter and more comforting."

---

在跟他人的交往中，我学到的重要一课是：不要过度表达不好意思，会让本来自然的关系变得沉重。“不好意思”、“麻烦你了”、“太辛苦你了”、“没必要这么做”这类话，偶尔说说是礼貌。但一直讲，强调亏欠和牺牲。会让对方也很难做。相当于人家在实质性的付出之外，还得再花精神安抚接收的人，反复证明的确是心甘情愿的，关系也变成了彼此欠债。认可付出有别的方式。换位来讲，当我为亲近的人做事，对方的快乐就是回报。在这个意义上，“实在是太麻烦了实在是太不好意思了”，倒不如一句“谢谢，你让我开心”来得轻盈舒心。
